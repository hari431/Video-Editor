OUT-OF-SEQUENCE TRANSACTIONS
============================

An out-of-sequence transaction occurs when a transaction is effective earlier than a transaction that was already bound later in the policy timeline.

CONCEPT
-------
Imagine a policy change was bound effective July 1, then another transaction is created later but needs an effective date of June 15. The second transaction is earlier in effective-date sequence.

WHY IT IS DIFFICULT
-------------------
The system must preserve the meaning of the policy timeline and determine how later transactions are affected.

PREEMPTION AND REWRITING
------------------------
When an OOS transaction binds, Guidewire may preempt (invalidate) the later-bound branch's slices after the OOS effective date, or rewrite them by replaying their edits on top of the new branch. Example:

Branch A (Bound 2026-07-01)  [Jan 01 ------- July01 --------------- Dec31)
Branch B (OOS 2026-06-15 later created, effective June15) [Jan01 -- June15 ------- Dec31)
After Branch B binds, Branch A's slices after June15 are preempted; its non-conflicting changes are re-applied onto Branch B's slices where possible. Rating is re-run.

REFERENCE GROUNDING
-------------------
The supplied PolicyCenter material defines out-of-sequence transactions in this general temporal sense and also discusses reinstatement scenarios.

PRACTICAL THINKING WITH DATES
-----------------------------
Always distinguish `CreateTime` vs `EditEffectiveDate` vs `SliceDate`. Code that sorts Jobs by creation order and assumes that is the policy order will misreport coverage at loss. Use:

var job = Job.finder.findJobByJobNumber("12345")
var oosEffective = job.LatestPeriod.EditEffectiveDate
var createdOn = job.CreateTime

Comparison `job.CreateTime > other.CreateTime` does not imply `EditEffectiveDate` ordering.

GOSU DETECTION PATTERN
----------------------
To list policy periods that may be OOS candidates for a given effective date:

uses gw.api.database.Query
var candidates = Query.make(entity.PolicyPeriod)
  .compare(PolicyPeriod#PolicyNumber, Equals, policyNumber)
  .compare(PolicyPeriod#EditEffectiveDate, GreaterThan, oosEffective)
  .compare(PolicyPeriod#Job, NotEquals, null)
  .select()

COMMON MISTAKES
---------------
Assuming the latest transaction created is always the latest effective transaction. Also quoting an OOS PolicyChange without re-quoting downstream preempted branches, leading to stale premium.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
-------------
Out-of-sequence reasoning is fundamentally about effective dates versus transaction order.

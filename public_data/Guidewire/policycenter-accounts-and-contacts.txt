POLICYCENTER ACCOUNTS AND CONTACTS
==================================

Accounts provide a business context around policy relationships, while contacts represent people or organizations involved in insurance activity.

ACCOUNT CONCEPT
---------------
An account can be associated with one or more policies and provides a place to manage relationships and account-level information.

CONTACTS
--------
Contacts may represent insured parties and other participants. The exact roles depend on configuration and product.

WHY SEPARATION MATTERS
----------------------
A person or organization may participate in more than one policy or role. Separating reusable party information from individual policy transactions reduces duplication.

ENTITY RELATIONSHIP AND REUSE
--------------------------------
Guidewire’s data model (simplified):

Account (1) ──< Policy (many) ──< PolicyPeriod (many Branch/Slice)
  │
  └──< AccountContact (link to Contact)
Contact (1) ──< AccountContact >── Account
Contact ──< ContactRole (Driver, AdditionalInsured, Lienholder)

A Contact is an `ABContact` subtype (Person or Company). Reusing the same Contact across two Policies via two AccountContacts avoids duplicating name/address, but each PolicyPeriod slice can still override contact address for that term.

GOSU LOOKUP EXAMPLE
-------------------
Finding all policies for an account number:

uses gw.api.database.Query
var policies = Query.make(entity.Policy)
  .join(Policy#Account)
  .compare(Account#AccountNumber, Equals, "10001234")
  .select()

Upstream code that filters `Contact` by `Account` directly (without `AccountContact` join) returns zero rows; the join must go through the link entity.

COMMON MISTAKES
---------------
Assuming an account is identical to a policy, or assuming every contact has the same business role. Also updating `Contact` address expecting all PolicyPeriod slices to change retroactively; effective-dated slices retain their historical address snapshot.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
-------------
Accounts provide relationship context; policies and jobs represent policy-specific insurance transactions.

WHAT IS INSURANCE
=================

Insurance is a risk-management arrangement in which an insurer accepts specified risks in exchange for consideration, usually premium, subject to the terms of a contract.

CORE IDEA
---------
The insured transfers specified financial risk to the insurer within defined policy terms. The insurer pools risks across many policyholders and uses underwriting, pricing and claims processes to manage the portfolio.

POLICY AS A CONTRACT
--------------------
A policy defines who is insured, what is covered, limits, deductibles, exclusions, conditions, dates and other obligations. Coverage is not simply a promise to pay every loss.

POOLING NUMERIC EXAMPLE
-----------------------
100 homeowners each pay $1,000 premium → $100,000 pool. In a year, 4 homes suffer $20,000 fire losses = $80,000. The pool funds those 4, with $20,000 remaining for expenses, reinsurance, and surplus. No single homeowner could absorb a $20,000 loss alone, but the pooled structure makes it bearable. Underwriting aims to price the $1,000 so the pool remains solvent across years.

EXAMPLE WITH COVERAGE LOGIC
---------------------------
A homeowner may purchase property and liability coverage. A covered loss is evaluated against the policy's definitions, exclusions, limits and deductible. Example claim $50,000 dwelling fire:

Limit $200,000 → loss within limit
Deductible $1,000 → insurer pays $49,000
Exclusion “earth movement” not triggered → covered
If loss were flood and policy excludes flood, payout $0 despite limit.

REFERENCE GROUNDING
-------------------

COMMON MISTAKES
---------------
Treating insurance as a savings account, assuming every loss is covered, and ignoring exclusions and conditions. Also comparing premium alone without comparing limits and deductibles across quotes.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
-------------
Insurance is structured risk transfer through a contract. Coverage depends on the policy terms, not simply on the fact that a loss occurred.

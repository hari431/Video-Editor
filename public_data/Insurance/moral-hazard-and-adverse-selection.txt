MORAL HAZARD AND ADVERSE SELECTION
==================================

Moral hazard and adverse selection describe different sources of insurance risk that can affect underwriting and portfolio results.

MORAL HAZARD
------------
Moral hazard concerns behavior that may increase loss frequency or severity, including intentional dishonesty in extreme cases.

ADVERSE SELECTION
-----------------
Adverse selection can occur when people with higher expected risk are more motivated to seek coverage while lower-risk people are less inclined to participate, depending on market design.

UNDERWRITING RESPONSE
---------------------
Insurers may use eligibility rules, pricing, underwriting questions, deductibles, limits, inspections and claims controls to manage these effects.

EXAMPLE
-------
If a market attracts disproportionately high-risk applicants because low-risk applicants opt out, the portfolio can become more expensive than expected.

COMMON MISTAKES
---------------
Treating the two terms as synonyms. Moral hazard concerns behavior after or around insurance; adverse selection concerns the composition of the insured risk pool.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
-------------
Both concepts affect portfolio quality, but they describe different mechanisms and require different controls.

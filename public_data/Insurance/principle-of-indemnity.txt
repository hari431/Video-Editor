PRINCIPLE OF INDEMNITY
======================

Indemnity is the principle that insurance compensation should generally restore the insured to the financial position intended by the contract rather than create a profit from the loss, subject to policy terms and product design.

VALUATION MATH EXAMPLE
----------------------
If covered property suffers a repairable loss, a claim payment is determined according to the applicable valuation basis, limits, deductible and policy conditions.

Dwelling roof fire: replacement cost to repair like-kind = $50,000
Depreciation (10 years, 20%) = $10,000
Actual cash value = $40,000
Policy limit $200,000 and deductible $1,000 → RC payout $49,000 (50k-1k); ACV payout $39,000 (40k-1k) if policy is ACV basis. Under-insurance penalty (co-insurance clause) could further reduce RC payout if building was insured to only 60% of value.

NOT UNIVERSAL AND BETTERMENT
----------------------------
Not every insurance product operates as a simple indemnity contract. Life insurance, for example, uses agreed benefits rather than repairing a measurable property loss. Betterment (e.g., replacing a worn 15-year roof with a brand-new 30-year roof) may be apportioned so the insured does not profit; the insurer restores function, not new-for-old in every case.

RELATED CONCEPTS COMPARISON
---------------------------
Replacement cost and actual cash value are different valuation approaches. Limits and deductibles also affect the final payment.

Basis       Pays                              When used          Example $50k loss $1k deductible
RC          Full repair cost within limit     Many homeowners   $49,000
ACV         RC minus depreciation             Older property    $39,000 (with $10k depreciation)
Stated Value Agreed cap (e.g., $300k)      Classic car       min(agreed, repair)
Life        Agreed benefit ($500k)            Life product     $500k regardless of loss size

COMMON MISTAKES
---------------
Assuming indemnity means every claim pays market value or that it applies identically to every insurance product.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
-------------
Indemnity is best understood within the type of insurance and the policy's valuation and benefit structure.

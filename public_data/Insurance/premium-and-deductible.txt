PREMIUM AND DEDUCTIBLE
======================

Premium is the amount charged for insurance coverage. A deductible is the portion of a covered loss that the insured generally bears before the insurer's payment, subject to policy terms.

PREMIUM DRIVERS
---------------
Pricing can consider exposure characteristics, expected losses, expenses, catastrophe risk, reinsurance, taxes/fees, competition and other regulatory or business factors.

DEDUCTIBLE EFFECT
-----------------
A higher deductible can reduce the insurer's payment for smaller losses and may affect premium, but the relationship is product- and market-specific.

EXAMPLE
-------
If a covered loss is valued at 10,000 and the applicable deductible is 1,000, the policy's covered payment may be 9,000 before considering other limits or conditions.

COMMON MISTAKES
---------------
Treating the deductible as an extra premium or assuming the example calculation applies when exclusions, limits or coinsurance change the result.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
-------------
Premium prices coverage; deductible allocates part of covered loss to the insured. Both are policy-specific.

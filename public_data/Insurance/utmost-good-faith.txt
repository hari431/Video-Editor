UTMOST GOOD FAITH
=================

Insurance contracts depend heavily on accurate disclosure and fair dealing because the insurer often prices a risk using information supplied by the applicant.

UNDERWRITING RELATIONSHIP
-------------------------
Applicants are expected to provide material information accurately. Insurers also have obligations defined by contract and law.

EXAMPLE
-------
If a material fact affecting eligibility or pricing is deliberately misrepresented, the consequences can be significant depending on jurisdiction and policy wording.

PRACTICAL IMPORTANCE
--------------------
Clear questions, documentation, verification and transparent communication help reduce disputes.

COMMON MISTAKES
---------------
Assuming every inaccurate answer automatically voids a policy or treating all misstatements as equally material.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
-------------
Good-faith disclosure is central to insurance because underwriting depends on reliable information. Legal consequences depend on the contract and jurisdiction.

PERIL AND HAZARD
================

A peril is a cause of loss, while a hazard is a condition that increases the likelihood or severity of loss.

PERIL
-----
Fire, theft, collision and certain natural events can be examples of perils depending on the insurance context.

HAZARD
------
A physical hazard is a physical condition that increases risk. A moral hazard can arise from behavior or dishonesty. A morale hazard describes carelessness or changed behavior because a person feels protected.

EXAMPLE
-------
Fire is a peril; faulty electrical wiring can be a physical hazard that increases the chance of fire.

WHY UNDERWRITING CARES
----------------------
Underwriters assess characteristics that affect expected loss and may respond through eligibility, pricing, deductibles, limits, exclusions or conditions.

COMMON MISTAKES
---------------
Calling every cause a hazard or every risky condition a peril.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
-------------
Perils cause losses; hazards influence the probability or severity of losses.
